Projected Delaunay triangulations

Points move around a box and bounce off its walls, each with the speed and direction it got at the start. Every frame, they are projected onto the three coordinate planes, and each projection is triangulated by a divide and conquer algorithm: xOy on the floor, yOz and zOx on the walls behind, set back from the box so they don't hide it. A projection's edges that are also edges of the 3D hull keep their colour; the others are faded. The edges of each projection's convex hull also join the points in the box, in the same colour. An edge on more than one hull mixes their colours as light does: yellow, magenta or cyan for two, white for all three (light grey on a light background). Those edges are silhouettes of the points' 3D convex hull, drawn in light facets tinted by the way they face. The hull itself comes from two weighted Delaunay triangulations of the xOy projection: with heights z they give its lower half, with heights −z its upper half. Drag to turn the box.
